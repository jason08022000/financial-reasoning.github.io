Introduction

We introduce FinMR, a novel multimodal benchmark designed to evaluate the reasoning capabilities of multimodal LLMs in financial problem-solving. Compared with prior works, this study features four key advancements. First, FinMR consists of 3200 college-level question-answer pairs—1049 involving financial math and 2151 probing financial expertise—each integrating textual and visual content, images ranging from financial tables to stock price trends. In particular, all answers have expert-annotated explanations, facilitating the analysis of reasoning errors and model underperformance. Second, to enhance financial reasoning in MLLMs, we propose Error Feedback Learning (EFL) by retrieving similar negative examples and error feedback for iterative self-improvement. Third, to enhance financial reasoning in MLLMs, we evaluate open-source and closed-source LLMs and MLLMs using Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and EFL strategies, demonstrating that MLLMs outperform standard LLMs, with EFL proving more effective than CoT. Finally, further error analysis highlights three primary challenges: image recognition failures, question misunderstanding, and incorrect formula adoption. By addressing these challenges, FinMR not only serves as a benchmark for future research but also provides a foundation for developing more advanced techniques to enhance financial reasoning capabilities.
